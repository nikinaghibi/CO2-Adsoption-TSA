# CO₂ Adsorption Using Temperature Swing Adsorption (TSA)

## Aspen HYSYS Process Simulation

This repository presents an academic process simulation project focused on the **capture and separation of carbon dioxide (CO₂) from a flue-gas stream using solid adsorption and Temperature Swing Adsorption (TSA)**.

The process was modeled in **Aspen HYSYS** to investigate the behavior of a packed adsorption column and to compare the performance of two solid adsorbents:

* **PEI-impregnated silica gel**
* **Activated carbon**

The project combines process simulation, adsorption theory, dynamic cycle design, and comparative performance analysis.

---

## Project Objectives

The main objectives of this project were to:

* Model CO₂ separation from a simplified flue-gas stream.
* Develop an adsorption/regeneration cycle based on **Temperature Swing Adsorption (TSA)**.
* Investigate the influence of adsorption time on CO₂ separation performance.
* Compare **PEI-functionalized silica gel** and **activated carbon** as potential adsorbents.
* Evaluate important performance indicators including:

  * CO₂ purity
  * CO₂ recovery
  * Adsorbent productivity
* Study the influence of adsorbent properties and operating conditions on column performance.

---

## Process Overview

The feed stream was modeled as a simplified flue gas containing:

| Component | Mole Fraction |
| --------- | ------------: |
| CO₂       |           13% |
| N₂        |           87% |

The adsorption system consists of a packed-bed adsorption column operating approximately at atmospheric pressure.

The TSA cycle is divided into three main operating stages:

### 1. Adsorption

The flue-gas stream enters the adsorption column from the bottom. CO₂ is preferentially retained by the solid adsorbent while the nitrogen-rich gas leaves the column.

Representative operating conditions:

* Pressure: **1 bar**
* Feed temperature: **95 °C**
* CO₂: **13 mol%**
* N₂: **87 mol%**
* Adsorption period: approximately **100 s**

---

### 2. Regeneration

After the adsorption stage, the saturated bed is regenerated by increasing its temperature.

Steam is introduced from the top of the column in counter-current operation to release the adsorbed CO₂ from the solid phase.

Representative regeneration conditions:

* Steam temperature: **150 °C**
* Regeneration period: approximately **250 s**
* Steam composition: **100% H₂O**

This step enables adsorbent regeneration and CO₂ recovery before the next adsorption cycle.

---

### 3. Cooling

Following regeneration, the adsorption bed must be returned to conditions suitable for the next adsorption cycle.

Nitrogen is introduced from the top of the column to cool and condition the adsorbent bed.

Representative cooling conditions:

* N₂ concentration: **100%**
* Cooling-gas temperature: approximately **105 °C**
* Cooling period: up to approximately **400 s**

The cooling stage continues until the bed approaches the target adsorption temperature.

## Adsorbents

Two adsorbents were investigated.

### PEI-Impregnated Silica Gel

PEI-functionalized silica provides enhanced interaction with CO₂ due to the presence of amine functional groups.

Representative properties used in the study include:

* Specific surface area: **845 m²/g**
* Density: **1216 kg/m³**
* Desorption temperature: **150 °C**

### Activated Carbon

Activated carbon was considered as a conventional porous adsorbent for comparison.

Representative properties include:

* Specific surface area: **800 m²/g**
* Density: **2100 kg/m³**
* Desorption temperature: **150 °C**

## Modeling Assumptions

The mathematical and simulation framework was developed using several simplifying assumptions:

* The gas phase is treated using ideal-gas behavior.
* Radial heat and mass dispersion are neglected.
* Axial dispersion is considered.
* Gas flow through the packed bed is represented using Darcy-type pressure behavior.
* Solid-phase mass-transfer kinetics are represented using a **Linear Driving Force (LDF)** model.
* Particle size is assumed uniform throughout the adsorption column.
* Adsorption equilibrium is represented using a **Langmuir-type adsorption isotherm**.

These assumptions provide a simplified framework for analyzing transient adsorption behavior in the packed bed.

---

## Process Control and Cycle Logic

Different valve configurations were defined for each TSA operating stage.

During adsorption:

* Feed valve: **Open**
* Top product valve: **Open**
* Bottom product valve: **Closed**
* Steam valve: **Closed**
* Cooling N₂ valve: **Closed**

During regeneration:

* Feed valve: **Closed**
* Top product valve: **Closed**
* Bottom product valve: **Open**
* Steam valve: **Open**
* Cooling N₂ valve: **Closed**

During cooling:

* Feed valve: **Closed**
* Top product valve: **Closed**
* Bottom product valve: **Open**
* Steam valve: **Closed**
* Cooling N₂ valve: **Open**

This switching sequence forms the basis of the dynamic TSA operating cycle.

## Performance Indicators

Three main performance metrics were considered.

### CO₂ Purity

CO₂ purity represents the fraction of recovered CO₂ relative to the total recovered CO₂ and N₂ after regeneration.

### CO₂ Recovery

Recovery represents the fraction of CO₂ recovered during regeneration relative to the amount of CO₂ introduced during the adsorption stage.

### CO₂ Productivity

Productivity evaluates the amount of recovered CO₂ relative to the adsorbent mass and the total TSA cycle duration.

Together, these parameters provide a basis for comparing adsorbent performance and identifying suitable operating conditions.

---

## Key Observations

The literature-based performance analysis incorporated into this project indicates that:

* CO₂ purity increases with increasing adsorption time.
* CO₂ recovery decreases as adsorption time becomes longer.
* Adsorption time therefore represents an important trade-off between product purity and recovery.
* PEI-impregnated silica gel demonstrates stronger CO₂ adsorption behavior than activated carbon under the investigated conditions.
* The presence of amine functional groups improves the affinity of the silica-based adsorbent toward CO₂.
* PEI-functionalized silica also demonstrates higher CO₂ productivity than activated carbon for the investigated TSA cycle.

These observations highlight the importance of adsorbent selection and cycle-time optimization when designing adsorption-based carbon-capture processes.

## Engineering Relevance

Adsorption-based carbon capture is a potential pathway for reducing CO₂ emissions from industrial processes and fossil-fuel-based energy systems.

Temperature Swing Adsorption enables cyclic adsorbent regeneration by exploiting the temperature dependence of adsorption equilibrium. Selection of an appropriate adsorbent, cycle configuration, and adsorption time is therefore essential for balancing CO₂ purity, recovery, productivity, and process energy requirements.

The project provides an academic investigation of these interactions through process simulation and comparison of two different adsorbent materials.

---

## Limitations

The project primarily focuses on process modeling, cycle development, and literature-based validation.

Because of simulation execution difficulties encountered during the final dynamic runs, some comparative performance plots discussed in the accompanying report were obtained from the reference study rather than generated directly by the HYSYS model.

Therefore, these plots should be interpreted as **literature-based validation and comparative analysis**, rather than independent simulation predictions.
